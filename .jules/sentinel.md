## 2025-02-27 - Fix Overly Permissive CORS Configuration
**Vulnerability:** The application was allowing requests from any origin by returning `callback(null, true)` as a fallback for unknown origins, despite setting `credentials: true`. This exposes the application to severe Cross-Origin Resource Sharing (CORS) attacks, potentially leading to unauthorized cross-origin access and data leakage, especially when authenticated routes rely on cookies or authorization headers. Both Express (`server/index.js`) and Socket.io (`server/services/socketService.js`) shared this vulnerability.
**Learning:** The soft permissive fallback was likely introduced for development convenience or ease of integrating new domains (extensions/external clients), prioritizing rapid iteration over strict boundary controls, without fully realizing the implications of combining permissive origins with `credentials: true`.
**Prevention:** Always enforce a strict whitelist for `allowedOrigins` in both Express middleware and WebSockets. Ensure that unknown origins explicitly return an error (`callback(new Error('Not allowed by CORS'))`) rather than silently passing. Avoid wildcard logic or soft-allow fallbacks in production configurations.
